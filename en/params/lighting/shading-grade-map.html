Shading Grade Map

Per-area shadow darkness control via grayscale texture

Overview

Shading Grade Map controls shadow darkness on a per-texel basis using a grayscale texture. White areas receive the full light calculation as normal, gray areas are biased toward shadow, and black areas are always rendered in shadow regardless of the lighting conditions. This gives artists precise control over shadow placement on specific body areas without having to change the lighting setup. Keyword: _SHADING_GRADE_MAP

Parameters

Shading Grade Map Texture _ShadingGradeMap texture

A grayscale texture that controls the shadow bias per texel. White (1.0) means the area uses normal lighting calculation. Gray values bias the area toward shadow. Black (0.0) forces the area into full shadow regardless of the light direction.

Default: "white"

Paint the grade map to control where shadows appear regardless of lighting. For example, paint cheek areas slightly gray to ensure they always have subtle shadow for a cute anime look. Use the same UV layout as the main texture.
Shading Grade Scale _ShadingGradeScale range

A scale/offset value applied to the grade map. Positive values brighten the grade map (less shadow overall), and negative values darken it (more shadow overall). This allows global adjustment of the grade map's influence without editing the texture.

Default: 0

Range: -1 to 1

Use this as a quick global adjustment. If the grade map produces too much shadow, increase the scale. If the shadows are too subtle, decrease it. Fine-tune per-area control in the texture itself.

Usage Tips

  • White = fully lit (normal lighting calculation), Black = fully shadowed (always in shadow). Gray values provide intermediate bias.
  • Paint cheek areas slightly gray (around 0.7-0.8) to create permanent subtle shadows that give a cute, soft anime appearance.
  • Paint neck and under-chin areas darker (0.3-0.5) to ensure these areas always have shadow, mimicking ambient occlusion.
  • The Scale parameter adjusts overall bias: positive values brighten (less shadow), negative values darken (more shadow).
  • This feature is commonly used in lilToon-compatible workflows. If you are migrating from lilToon, your existing shading grade maps should work directly.
  • Combine with the standard shadow controls for layered shadow artistry — the grade map biases the lighting calculation before other shadow effects are applied.
