Light Snap

Quantize light direction to prevent shadow flickering

Overview

Light Snap quantizes the light direction vector to discrete angle intervals, preventing shadow flickering caused by subtle light direction changes. This is particularly useful in real-time lighting environments and VRChat worlds where light directions may shift slightly, causing toon shadow boundaries to flicker.

Internally, the light direction is converted to spherical coordinates (phi, theta), rounded to the nearest multiple of the snap angle, and then blended back with the original direction using the smoothness parameter.

Controlled by the keyword _LIGHT_SNAP. When disabled, no shader instructions are generated.

Parameters

Enable Light Snap _LightSnap toggle

Toggles the Light Snap feature on or off. When enabled, the light direction is snapped to discrete angular intervals.

Default: 0 (disabled)

Snap Angle _LightSnapAngle range

The angular unit (in degrees) to which the light direction is snapped. For example, setting 45 degrees quantizes the light to 8 directions (360/45). Smaller values create finer snapping (smoother but less stable), while larger values create coarser snapping (more stable but potentially unnatural).

Default: 45

Range: 5 to 90 (degrees)

Recommended: 15-45 degrees. For VRChat worlds, 30-45 degrees provides a good balance between stability and naturalness.
Snap Smoothness _LightSnapSmooth range

Controls the smoothness of the snap transition. 0 uses the fully snapped direction (maximum stability, stepped transitions), while 1 uses the original light direction (no snapping). Internally computed as lerp(lightDir, snappedDir, 1.0 - smooth).

Default: 0.1

Range: 0 to 1

Low values of 0.05-0.2 are recommended. This maintains snap stability while minimizing stepping artifacts during direction transitions.

Usage Tips

  • VRChat usage: In VRChat worlds, player movement can cause the light direction to change slightly, resulting in flickering toon shadow boundaries. Light Snap stabilizes these shadows.
  • Cel-shading compatibility: Hard cel-shading (binary shadow) is especially sensitive to small light direction changes, making this feature particularly effective in such setups.
  • Combining with SDF Shadow: When used together with SDF-based face shadows, facial lighting becomes even more stable, preventing expression distortion.
  • Caution: If the snap angle is too large, shadows may visibly jump when the light direction crosses a snap boundary. Use the smoothness parameter to mitigate this.
