Procedural AO

Automatic height-based ambient occlusion generation

Overview

Procedural AO (Procedural Ambient Occlusion) automatically generates an AO gradient based on the object-space Y coordinate (height). This allows you to add natural darkening to the lower parts of a model (such as feet, hems, and skirt bottoms) without the need to prepare an AO map texture. It is multiplied with the existing AO map (_AOMap) when both are enabled, allowing you to combine them for richer shadow detail.

Controlled by the keyword _PROCEDURAL_AO. When disabled, no shader instructions are generated.

Parameters

Enable Procedural AO _ProceduralAO toggle

Toggles the Procedural AO feature on or off. When enabled, AO is automatically calculated from the object-space height information.

Default: 0 (disabled)

AO Height Offset _ProceduralAOHeightOffset range

Offsets the base height of the AO gradient. Positive values expand the AO region upward, while negative values shrink it downward. Adjust according to your model's pivot position and scale.

Default: 0

Range: -2 to 2

If the model's pivot is at the feet, use a positive value to expand the effect range. If the pivot is at the center, use a negative value to apply AO only to the lower half.
AO Intensity _ProceduralAOIntensity range

Controls the strength of the Procedural AO effect. 0 completely disables the effect (everything remains bright), while 1 applies maximum darkening. Internally computed as lerp(1.0, proceduralAO, intensity).

Default: 0.5

Range: 0 to 1

Recommended: 0.3-0.6. Values that are too high may cause the ground area to appear unnaturally dark. Start conservative and adjust upward.
AO Softness _ProceduralAOSoftness range

Controls the softness of the AO gradient boundary. Higher values create a wider, smoother transition, while lower values create a sharper boundary. Used internally as the range parameter for smoothstep.

Default: 0.5

Range: 0.01 to 2

For toon-style rendering, 0.3-0.8 works well. Values close to the minimum (0.01) produce a crisp, cel-shading style AO edge.

Usage Tips

  • Texture-free quick AO: Even for models where baking AO maps is difficult, you can add natural shading based on height. Ideal for low-poly models and quick prototypes.
  • Combining with AO Map: Since it multiplies with the existing AO map (_AOMap), you can use it to supplement broad environmental occlusion that is difficult to express with texture-based AO alone.
  • Clothing hems and shoes: Adjust the Height Offset to add natural gradient shadows to skirt hems, boot soles, glove wrists, and similar areas.
  • Performance: Since no texture sampling is performed, this is lighter than texture-based AO maps. Effective for mobile/Quest platform optimization.
