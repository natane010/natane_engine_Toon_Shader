Tessellation

Hardware tessellation and displacement mapping for surface detail

Overview

Tessellation adds hardware-level geometry subdivision to your mesh at render time, creating smoother silhouettes and rounder surfaces without modifying the source mesh. By subdividing triangles on the GPU, edges that would appear jagged or faceted become smooth curves.

Optional displacement mapping takes this further by extruding the subdivided geometry based on a heightmap texture, producing real geometric detail such as bumps, cracks, and surface relief that responds correctly to lighting and silhouette edges.

Controlled by the keywords _TESSELLATION and _TESS_DISPLACEMENT.

Parameters

Tessellation Factor _TessFactor range

Controls the subdivision level applied to each triangle. A factor of 4 splits each triangle into roughly 16 smaller triangles. Higher values produce smoother geometry but significantly increase the triangle count.

Default: 4

Range: 1 - 16

A factor of 16 generates approximately 256 triangles per original triangle. Keep this value as low as possible while achieving the desired smoothness.
Phong Tessellation Strength _TessPhongStrength range

Applies Phong tessellation smoothing, which projects subdivided vertices onto the curved surface defined by vertex normals. This rounds out flat-shaded geometry without a displacement map.

Default: 0.5

Range: 0 - 1

At 1.0, vertices are fully projected to the interpolated normal plane. Use 0.3-0.7 for natural-looking curvature on character models.
Normal Smoothing _TessNormalSmooth range

Controls the interpolation smoothing applied to normals on the subdivided surface. Higher values produce softer shading transitions across tessellated faces.

Default: 0.5

Range: 0 - 1

Distance Min _TessDistanceMin float

The near distance threshold in world units. Objects closer than this distance receive the full tessellation factor. This is measured from the camera to the vertex.

Default: 1

Distance Max _TessDistanceMax float

The far distance threshold in world units. Objects beyond this distance receive no tessellation (factor = 1). Between Min and Max, the factor is linearly interpolated.

Default: 20

Setting appropriate distance limits is essential for performance. Most viewers won't notice tessellation beyond 10-15 meters.
Displacement Map _TessDispMap texture

A grayscale heightmap texture used to displace the tessellated vertices along their normals. White areas are pushed outward, black areas are pushed inward (or vice versa depending on strength sign).

Default: "gray"

Use a mid-gray (128) base for displacement maps so detail can extrude both inward and outward.
Displacement Strength _TessDispStrength range

Controls the amplitude of the displacement effect. Positive values push geometry outward along normals, negative values push inward. Zero disables displacement entirely.

Default: 0

Range: -1 - 1

Displacement Offset _TessDispOffset range

Shifts the center point of the displacement. At 0, mid-gray in the heightmap produces no displacement. Adjusting this value biases the displacement inward or outward overall.

Default: 0

Range: -0.5 - 0.5

Usage Tips

  • Performance warning: TessFactor of 16 generates up to 16x more triangles per mesh. Always use distance-based LOD (TessDistanceMin/Max) to limit tessellation to close-up views only.
  • VRChat Quest: Avoid tessellation entirely on Quest platform. Mobile GPUs do not handle hardware tessellation efficiently, and it may cause severe frame drops.
  • Combine Phong tessellation with normal maps for the best visual result at moderate subdivision levels (4-6).
  • Displacement maps should match your normal map for consistent surface detail.
  • For character models, tessellation is most effective on silhouette edges (arms, head outline). Consider using it selectively on key materials only.
